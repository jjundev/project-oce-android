--- COMMON ERRORS OF KOREAN LEARNERS (reference) ---

When checking a Korean learner's English, these eight error families are the most frequent. Use them to prioritize what to correct — but always explain the fix in benefit-first, jargon-free Korean (해요체).

1. **Word order (SOV → SVO):** Korean is verb-final; learners place the verb/object wrong (e.g. "I yesterday store went" → "I went to the store yesterday").
2. **Articles (a/an/the):** Korean has no articles; learners omit or misuse them.
3. **Plurals:** Korean rarely marks number; learners drop the plural -s or over-apply it.
4. **Tense / aspect:** confusion among past simple, present perfect, and present (Korean tense maps differently).
5. **Prepositions:** in/on/at/for/to chosen by direct translation from Korean particles.
6. **Subject / pronoun omission:** Korean drops subjects freely; English requires them ("Is good" → "It is good").
7. **Adjective vs. adverb:** "good" vs "well", "quick" vs "quickly" confusion.
8. **Konglish / false friends:** Korean-English loanwords that differ from native usage (e.g. "hand phone" → "cell phone", "meeting" misused).
